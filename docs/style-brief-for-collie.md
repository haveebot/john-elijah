# John Elijah — style brief for Collie (2026-09-03)

Winston's framing: **John is the Texas-coast version of Marcus King and Eddie 9V** — personality, style, music, guitar. This is what that means visually, what's already live on the site, and where the logo and package should land so everything reads as one act.

## The two references, decoded

**Marcus King (Greenville SC → Nashville).** Fourth-generation player, the cherry 1962 ES-345 ("Big Red") as a family heirloom, Rick Rubin and Dan Auerbach records. Visual world: wide-brim flat hats and tinted glasses, vintage western and 70s soul wardrobe, warm film-grade photography with deep shadow, serif and retro-script type on the covers, a lot of cherry red and tobacco brown. The brand reads *heritage soul-rock, expensive but worn-in*. His merch is embroidered caps, bandanas, picks: small, tactile, Southern.

**Eddie 9V (Atlanta).** Started at 15 on the Atlanta club circuit, cut *Capricorn* at the Allman Brothers' studio in Macon. Visual world: 60s R&B suits and open collars, Stax/Muscle Shoals nostalgia, grainy live photography, bold condensed vintage sans plus a script flourish, red-orange and cream. The brand reads *the kid who walked out of 1967 with a Telecaster*. Loud, joyful, a little unpolished on purpose.

**What they share:** live-in-a-room records, a guitar that's a character, a hat or a look you can draw in two lines, type that feels printed not designed, and photography that looks like it was shot on a stage, not in a studio.

## John's version — the coastal read

The photos already give us the difference: John plays in **island shirts and western snap-shirts under stage sails**, red-gold hair, a sunburst semi-hollow, gulf light. He's not Nashville leather or Atlanta sharkskin. He's **salt air, brass stage light, sunset water**.

This is the Eddie 9V pairing (condensed vintage sans + script) with Marcus King's restraint.

## What the logo should do

- **One mark you can draw in two lines.** Think a star, a wave, or a guitar headstock silhouette — a *fragment* we can animate as light on the homepage (the motion piece forms the mark out of stage light; it needs a simple silhouette to work).
- **A wordmark that survives at 24px** in a nav bar and at 3 feet on a banner. Condensed caps with real letterspacing; a script "John" over a block "ELIJAH" is one honest route (the cover already hints at it).
- **Two-color rule:** cream on warm black, brass as the only accent. It has to sit next to the Lone Star shield without fighting it (their red-white-gold shield is loud; ours is quiet and warm).
- **Print first.** Koozies, caps, a screen-printed tee, a 4×6 sticker. If it needs gradients it's wrong.

## Photography direction (Jacob Vachon is already shooting this)

Keep: stage sails, string lights, sunset behind the band, the green-and-brass night frames, crowd from behind. Add: one clean portrait series in daylight by the water (the "hat" shot — whatever John's signature is: the cap, the shades, the shirt), and a studio-floor shot with the band for the EPK.

## Merch line (Lone Star conversation)

Start where the references start: **cap** (structured, brass thread), **koozie** (the Gruene Hall model — see the Lone Star graphic brief), **screen-print tee** (one color on black or cream), **sticker**. All four use only the mark + wordmark. This is the package that makes the merch-split ask to Lone Star concrete.
